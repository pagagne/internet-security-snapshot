Mission

Artificial intelligence has officially entered the cybersecurity fight. No one can say exactly when it started, but 2026 unleashed an unprecedented wave of global data breaches and AI-driven attacks, and experts warn that 2027 will escalate even faster and at a much greater scale. The mission is to measure the internet's vulnerability lifecycle and plot the cybersecurity shift quarter by quarter, using free, public reporting services from the US and around the world.

The Internet Security Snapshot tracks the health of internet security through 2027. Every quarter it answers one critical question: are defenders closing internet-exposed flaws faster than attackers can exploit them, and how is AI changing the race?

At its core, it answers using only free, public data, under a plan pre-registered with the Open Science Framework (OSF, DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/3XT82, 20 October 2026), so the findings cannot be shaped to fit a conclusion.

The study tracks ten core datasets. Seven are collected now:

  1. Flaw records: public records of software flaws, from CVE.org and NVD, plus CISA's Vulnrichment analysis.
  2. Known-exploited flaws: flaws confirmed as attacked in the wild, from CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog.
  3. Exploit likelihood: daily exploitation-probability scores, from EPSS.
  4. Fix dates: when vendors released fixes, from vendor advisories.
  5. Ransomware postings: victim claims on leak sites, counted only (no victim names), from ransomware.live (with written permission, 27 October 2026).
  6. Breach filings: material cybersecurity incidents disclosed by companies, from SEC filings.
  7. AI credits: AI tools credited in flaw records.

Three are pending permission:

  1. Exploited flaws, second source: independent exploitation evidence, from VulnCheck.
  2. Exposure scans: how many internet systems are still exposed to a flaw, from Shadowserver.
  3. Honeypot hits: real-world attack observations, from Shadowserver's honeypots.

Yearly cross-check: internet scan data for the exposure reference. Censys declined research access on 10 Nov 2026; the author decides at the 15 March 2027 review whether to reapply, license it, or drop it.

International views, being tested: national alerts on actively exploited flaws from France (CERT-FR) and Japan (JPCERT/CC); routing security by country from APNIC Labs (with written permission, 1 November 2026); regional splits of web traffic and ransomware postings; and data-breach notifications from the UK (ICO) and Australia (OAIC).

Each quarter, an automatic GitHub job freezes an exact copy of the data, the author checks by hand what automation cannot judge, and the seven pre-registered questions are reported: a provisional evaluation on the 1st of January, April, July and October, and a final one a month later. Q4 2026 is a labelled pilot; Q1 2027 is the first scored quarter. Additional context comes from news reporting, internet traffic data from Cloudflare Radar, and open repositories of software code and AI models and datasets.

Reports

The first report, for the Q4 2026 pilot, is due in early January 2027; each quarter then has a provisional report and a final report one month later. Until then this site shows the study's run health only.

Data sources and credits

The study uses public and permissioned data. Every source whose data it uses is listed here, credited as its terms or its written permission ask; none of them endorses the study or its findings.

Core data (scored)

  • CVE records: the CVE Program (cve.org), via the CVE List repository. https://www.cve.org/Legal/TermsOfUse Copyright © 1999-2026, The MITRE Corporation. CVE and the CVE logo are registered trademarks of The MITRE Corporation. Used under the CVE Program terms of use; CVE records are not altered.
  • Vulnerability details: the National Vulnerability Database (NIST). https://nvd.nist.gov/ This product uses the NVD API but is not endorsed or certified by the NVD.
  • Known exploited vulnerabilities: CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog. https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog
  • Exploitation flags and added analysis: CISA Vulnrichment (published in the CVE records). https://github.com/cisagov/vulnrichment
  • Second exploited list: VulnCheck KEV (community edition). https://vulncheck.com/kev (figures from 2026-12-01)
  • Exploit likelihood scores: FIRST EPSS (Exploit Prediction Scoring System). https://www.first.org/epss/
  • Fix dates in part from Red Hat security data, licensed CC BY 4.0. https://access.redhat.com/security/data
  • Fix dates and version data in part from OSV.dev and deps.dev (each record under its source database's licence). https://osv.dev/
  • Fix dates in part from the GitHub Advisory Database (reviewed advisories, CC BY 4.0). https://github.com/advisories
  • Fix dates in part from vendors' own security advisories (Microsoft, Cisco, Palo Alto Networks, Siemens) and Linux kernel fixing commits (git.kernel.org). https://git.kernel.org/
  • Company breach filings: US Securities and Exchange Commission, EDGAR. https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/
  • Source: Ransomware.live https://www.ransomware.live/

  • Data provided by GreyNoise. https://www.greynoise.io/
  • AI tool and model counts: public data from GitHub and Hugging Face, read through their official APIs; individuals' projects are never named. https://huggingface.co/
