# Pilot witness: role summary

**Internet Security Snapshot** - an independent, self-funded, non-commercial study that asks
every quarter through 2027 whether defenders are fixing exploitable software flaws faster than
attackers start using them, and how AI is changing that race. It uses only free public data, and
its plan was registered publicly before any data was collected (OSF, DOI
10.17605/OSF.IO/3XT82), so the results can't be bent to fit a story.

**Open now. Apply by 7 December 2026.** About **9 to 12 hours** in all, spread over
**7 December 2026 to 5 February 2027**. Fully remote. One person.

## What the role is

An independent observer. Before the study collects real data, it runs a full test of the whole
process, then its first real quarter. The witness watches both and reports plainly what they
see: you read the run logs and results, ask questions, and note anything unclear, broken or
different from the published plan. You don't write code or collect data. A careful, skeptical
eye matters more than any background, and having no stake in the outcome is the point.

## What you would do

1. **9-14 December 2026, getting started (2 to 3 hours).** Read a short overview and the plan,
   and one video call to walk through how the study works.
2. **15-16 December, watch the full test run (about 2 hours).** Follow the dry run of the whole
   data collection through its public logs.
3. **1 January - 1 February 2027, watch the pilot quarter (4 to 5 hours).** The automated
   collection on 1 January (its logs and a frozen copy of the data), a sample of the January
   hand checks, and the final run on 1 February.
4. **By 5 February, write a short note (1 to 2 hours).** A page or two on any problems you saw,
   and on whether the procedures are ready to lock. Published under your name as a citable
   document.

## Who it suits

Graduate or advanced undergraduate students in statistics, data science, computer science,
information systems, public policy or a related field, or anyone who reads data and procedures
skeptically. No coding or security background is needed.

## What you get

- Named as the pilot witness in the pilot report, and listed as a contributor on its archived,
  citable release (Zenodo, with your ORCID iD if you have one).
- A reference letter or LinkedIn recommendation on request after your note.
- Course credit may be possible through your own department. The role is unpaid.

## How to apply

By email, open to everyone: write to
[internetsecuritysnapshot@gmail.com](mailto:internetsecuritysnapshot@gmail.com?subject=Pilot+witness)
with the subject "Pilot witness" and a few lines on why the role interests you and any relevant
coursework or experience. A resume is welcome but not required. If your school's Handshake shows
the posting "Pilot Witness for the Internet Security Snapshot", you can apply there instead; both
routes are treated the same. Applications close **7 December 2026**; the witness is confirmed by
9 December.

The witness and the independent reviewer ([`INVITATION.md`](INVITATION.md)) are always two
different people, and they never work together, so the reviewer starts fresh. The third role,
the second checker, is described in [`SECOND_CODER.md`](SECOND_CODER.md).
