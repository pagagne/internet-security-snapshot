## Posting 1: Pilot witness (post now)

**Pilot Witness for the Internet Security Snapshot**

I am looking for a pilot witness for a research project that starts collecting data this winter:
about 9 to 12 hours between 7 December 2026 and 5 February 2027, spread out, fully remote.

The role needs someone who reads data and procedures skeptically, cares more about whether a
process is sound than whether it looks good, and has no stake in the outcome. No security
background is needed.

**About the project**

The Internet Security Snapshot asks, every quarter through 2027, whether defenders are fixing
exploitable software flaws faster than attackers start using them, and how artificial
intelligence is changing that race. It uses only free public data, and the plan was registered
publicly in advance, so the results cannot be bent to fit a story.

- Registered plan (Open Science Framework): https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/3XT82
- Code and data: https://github.com/pagagne/internet-security-snapshot

**What you would do**

- Getting started, 7-14 December 2026 (2 to 3 hours, including one video call).
- Witness the full dry run on 15-16 December (about 2 hours).
- Witness the pilot quarter: the automated data collection on 1 January (through its public run
  logs and the frozen copy of the data), a sample of the January hand checks, and the final run
  on 1 February (about 4 to 5 hours).
- Write a short note by 5 February on any problems you saw and on the timing of locking the
  study's procedures (1 to 2 hours). Your note is published, under your name, as a citable
  document.

**Who should apply**

Graduate or advanced undergraduate students in statistics, data science, computer science,
information systems, public policy or a related field.

**Compensation and credit**

This is an unpaid volunteer role. You will be named as the pilot witness in the pilot report and
listed as a contributor on its archived, citable release (Zenodo, with your ORCID iD if you have
one). A reference letter or LinkedIn recommendation on request after the note. Course credit may
be possible through your own department.

**How to apply**

Send a short note on why the role interests you and any relevant coursework or experience.
Applications close 30 November 2026; the witness is confirmed by 6 December 2026.

Handshake form: Remote; Part time, hours blank; Temporary; start 12/07/2026, end 02/05/2027;
Unpaid; perk Career development.

## Posting 2: Independent reviewer (draft; goes live 4 Jan 2027)

**Independent Reviewer for the Internet Security Snapshot**

I am looking for an independent reviewer for a research project running through 2027: about 15
to 19 hours over roughly 14 months, more than half of it in April 2027.

The role needs someone who reads data and calculations skeptically, cares more about whether a
number is right than whether it tells a good story, and has no stake in the outcome. No security
background is needed.

**About the project**

The Internet Security Snapshot asks, every quarter, whether defenders are fixing exploitable
software flaws faster than attackers start using them, and how artificial intelligence is
changing that race. It uses only free public data, and the plan was registered publicly in
advance, so the results cannot be bent to fit a story.

- Project website: https://pagagne.github.io/internet-security-snapshot/ (live from 4 Dec)
- Registered plan (Open Science Framework): https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/3XT82
- Code and data: https://github.com/pagagne/internet-security-snapshot
- What the reviewer's work looks like (Reviewer's Desk): https://pagagne.github.io/internet-security-snapshot/reviewer/ (live from 4 Dec)

**What you would do**

- Independent review of the first scored quarter (April 2027): read the provisional report
  against the registered plan, recompute three randomly drawn headline figures from worksheets,
  and check a sample of hand decisions. You write a short review, which is published.
- Second coder (each quarter, about 45 minutes): answer 20 sampled decisions blind, mostly
  looking up when a vendor released a fix, on a simple web page. Your answers measure how often
  two careful people agree.

**Time and format**

- About 15 to 19 hours over 14 months; 8 to 12 of them between 3 and 28 April 2027.
- Getting started in early March 2027 (3 to 4 hours, including one video call).
- Fully remote; work at any time of day within each window.
- Runs from 2 March 2027 to 11 February 2028 (an optional comment on the annual check until
  14 March 2028).

**Who should apply**

Graduate or advanced undergraduate students in statistics, data science, economics, computer
science, public policy or a related field. Careful, skeptical reading of numbers matters more
than any security knowledge.

**Compensation and credit**

This is an unpaid volunteer role. You will be named as the independent reviewer in the published
reports and listed as a contributor on the archived, citable releases (Zenodo, with your ORCID
iD if you have one). Your written review is a citable document. A reference letter or LinkedIn
recommendation on request after the April 2027 review. Course credit may be possible through
your own department.

**How to apply**

Send a short note on why the role interests you and any relevant coursework or experience.
Applications close 15 February 2027; the reviewer is confirmed by 1 March 2027.
